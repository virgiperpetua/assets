About

Management first, engineering second — the same instinct.

I run a Gelato Messina store in Melbourne, which means most of my day is spent reading a floor: what's running low, what's slow, what's about to go wrong. I'm finishing an IT degree at Kaplan Business School alongside it, and the two have started to blend — I keep noticing that the operational problems I solve with a roster or a spreadsheet are usually better solved with software.

That's where the gelato production tool came from, and it's the lens I bring to full-stack engineering: build the thing that removes a real friction, not the thing that looks impressive.

A short detour

In my first trimester back in study, I failed a subject over a mistake I didn't catch in time. It was a genuine disappointment. I retook it the next trimester and finished it as one of my best results. It's not the headline of my degree, but it's a fair summary of how I work: something goes wrong, I look at why, and I come back better prepared.

What pulls my attention

AI, automation, and people

AI shows up in almost everything now, and I want to understand it well enough to use it properly — not as a buzzword, but as a genuine way to make useful software faster and better. That curiosity is the direction I'm building toward over the next few years, on top of the full-stack foundation I already have.

Outside of work and study: I'm fairly extroverted, I like a good film and a good playlist, and I have a dog who has strong opinions about my work-from-home days. None of that runs the business, but it's part of why I like building things for people, not just for a spec.
